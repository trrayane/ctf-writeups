About the library

The Old Quarter Public Library was founded in 1897 in a converted notary's office on the corner of Rue des Archives and Place du Vieux Marché. The original reading room is still in service, though the gas lamps have been replaced and the cat is younger.

Collection

Roughly 200,000 volumes across literature, history, the sciences, and a much-loved children's section. The bulk of the catalogue is in French and English, with smaller collections in Spanish, Russian, and Arabic. The rare-books room (visits by appointment) houses a small archive of 19th-century pamphlets and one slightly damp incunabulum.

Membership

Free for residents of the arrondissement. Visitors may obtain a one-month reading pass for €10 at the front desk. Children's cards are issued free of charge with a guardian's signature.

Accessibility

The ground floor and reading room are step-free. A platform lift serves the first floor and the rare-books room; please ask at the desk. Large-print and audiobook collections are kept on the ground floor near the periodicals.

Staff

We are a team of seven librarians and three archivists, supported by a rotating cast of volunteers and one elderly tabby called M. Pamuk.

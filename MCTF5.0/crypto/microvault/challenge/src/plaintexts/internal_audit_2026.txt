A note from the audit team. Late 2026. Filed under
internal-only and not for distribution beyond the
people already on this thread.

The cryptography team has asked the audit team to
confirm that Microvault remains functional as of the
close of the quarter, and to flag any operational
concerns that surfaced during the walkthrough. This
memo is the answer to both questions, written in the
order the questions were asked, with as little
ceremony as we could manage. The longer the document
the less anyone reads it.

Microvault is functional. The on-call engineer ran
through the daily checklist with the audit team and
nothing surprised either party. Files round-trip,
the MCP debug interface answers within milliseconds,
the access log shows the expected pattern of nobody
reading anything. We have no doubt the service is
operationally healthy on the day this memo was filed.

The first concern is the standing one. The encryption
primitive that protects files at rest is maintained
in-house by the cryptography team and has not been
reviewed by anyone outside the team. The cryptography
team has acknowledged this concern in every prior
audit cycle, and the recommendation to migrate to a
vetted authenticated-encryption mode (such as AES-GCM
or ChaCha20-Poly1305) appears in every report we have
filed against this service. The current cycle is no
exception. We continue to recommend migration. We
note again that the cryptography team has accepted
the risk pending migration and that the recommendation
should not be read as an ongoing dispute.

The second concern is operational. The MCP debug
interface was inadvertently bound to a public network
interface during the most recent deployment. The team
responsible has been notified. Until the binding is
corrected, the interface should be treated as
publicly reachable. The audit team verified that the
interface exposes only the two read-only tools that
the runbook documents (asset enumeration and asset
retrieval) and that nothing else is reachable through
that surface. Earlier revisions of the service did
expose a third diagnostic tool that disclosed
implementation details about the encryption layer;
the cryptography team removed that tool prior to
this audit.

The third item is not so much a concern as an
observation. The keying schedule that Microvault uses
for encryption-at-rest binds protected artefacts to
one another in a chain. The cryptography team has
confirmed that recovery of any single artefact's
plaintext is sufficient to compute the keying
parameters of the artefact downstream of it in that
chain. The audit team flags this as a blast-radius
concern but does not, at this audit, recommend a
specific change. We expect to revisit this in a
future cycle once the migration to a vetted primitive
has been planned in detail.

The fourth item is recommendations carried over from
prior audits. Per-file key derivation (HKDF from a
master secret and a file identifier), integrity
protection on every artefact (MAC at rest and in
transit), and a formal cipher-review process for any
future homegrown primitives. None of these have moved
since the last audit. We continue to recommend them.

Action items, by owner: DevOps lead to rebind the MCP
interface to loopback by end of Q4; cryptography lead
to begin the migration design by the start of Q1; the
audit team to schedule the follow-up cycle for Q2 of
the next year. The action items above are the only
items that the audit team will track between cycles.
Anything else is operational.

A closing note. The audit team thanks the DevOps and
cryptography teams for their cooperation during this
cycle. No issues were observed with willingness to
provide source, configuration, or context. The
recommendations above are made in good faith and
should not be interpreted as a criticism of the
engineering work performed.

The next audit of Microvault is scheduled for Q2 of
the next year and will revisit the recommendations
above.

-- end of memo --
